---
title: Networking and other players
description: What players receive about each other, and how other players appear in game.
---

## Range

The server only tells each player about the players **near them**:

- Within **150 m**, a player's position, look and clothes are sent. When someone comes into range, everything about them is sent at once, from the server's cache.
- Beyond **170 m**, they are removed. The gap between the two distances stops players at the edge from appearing and vanishing over and over.

Players far away cost no bandwidth and no performance. Positions are only sent to players in range; looks and clothes are sent once, when someone comes into range or changes them.

Names, chat and joins and leaves still reach everyone.

## How other players appear

Each player in range is drawn as a **stand-in**: the game's own mannequin, which has no AI.

- It wears the player's look and **the clothes they really wear**. When the player changes clothes in their inventory, the change reaches everyone in range straight away.
- Movement is smoothed between updates, and the stand-in walks, runs or sprints to match the player's speed.
- The player's name is drawn above it.

Clothes need no scripting. The game reports equipment changes to the platform, which sends the new outfit to the server, which passes it to the players in range.

<Callout type="info">
  Stand-ins have no physics, so players can walk through each other.
</Callout>

## What scripts see

- On the server, `GetPlayers`, `GetPlayerPosition` and the other player functions cover **every** player, whatever their range.
- On a client, `GetPlayerAppearance(id)` only knows players who have been in range, and `playerAppearanceChanged` fires when one comes into range. There is no client function that turns a player ID into their stand-in entity.
